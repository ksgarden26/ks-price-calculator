October Garden Update: Get Ready for Winter

October is a great time to prepare the garden for the colder months while there is still some warmth in the soil.

  • Clear heavy falls of leaves from lawns
  • Give hedges a final tidy where needed
  • Plant spring-flowering bulbs
  • Check tree stakes and ties
  • Aerate and scarify lawns where conditions allow
  • Leave useful stems, seed heads and shelter for wildlife
KS Garden Services Tip

An autumn tidy doesn’t mean stripping the garden bare. Keep useful structure and wildlife habitat while removing anything likely to cause problems through winter.

October 2026

Monty Don prepares to say goodbye to Gardeners’ World

Monty Don has announced that the current series of Gardeners’ World will be his last after 23 years as the programme’s lead presenter.

Viewers will continue to see Monty broadcasting from Longmeadow until later this year, bringing one of British gardening television’s best-known chapters to a close.

Monty has also revealed that the BBC has contributed towards the cost of two full-time gardeners who help keep Longmeadow ready for filming.

With that contribution ending after his departure, he has said he will have to find ways of generating income from the garden if he is to continue employing them.

After around 700 programmes, it really will be the end of an era for Gardeners’ World.

PLANT OF THE MONTH

October 2026

October Plant of the Month: Common Ivy

Hedera helix

Common ivy is one of our familiar British native plants and is often cut back before people get the chance to see just how valuable mature ivy can be for wildlife.

Most people see ivy getting large and immediately reach for the hedge cutter, but allowing at least some mature growth to flower can be hugely beneficial.

Its small greenish-yellow flowers appear in autumn when many other plants have finished flowering.

They provide an important late source of nectar and pollen for honeybees, bumblebees, hoverflies and other insects.

Leave it a little longer and the flowers develop into dark berries, providing another valuable food source — this time for garden birds during the colder months.

🐝
Don’t be too quick to cut it all back

Flowers for the bees in autumn, followed by berries for the birds in winter.

KS Garden Services Tip

Ivy can still be kept tidy without removing everything. Where possible, leave some mature flowering sections and selectively trim the rest. You get a tidier garden while still leaving a valuable food source for wildlife.
